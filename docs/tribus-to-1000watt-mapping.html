01How to read this

The new mockup is a single, editorial homepage — a magazine-style narrative (Hero → Buyers Now → The People → Lifestyle → Stories → How We Work → GroupGives → Search) rather than the utility-portal structure of the current site. The Fort Collins site is a TRIBUS/IDX platform organized around menus of tasks (Search, Buy, Sell, Market Data, About); the new one is organized around a feeling and a short set of moves.

That mismatch is the whole job. This framework does three things: it gives every current content bucket a single disposition (what happens to it), it names the orphans (content the new design has no slot for), and it names the net-new content the new design invents and will need written from scratch. Use the rubric in §2 to classify any page not yet listed — it's meant to outlive this first pass.

Comparison note: this is the Fort Collins counterpart to the Steamboat framework, and the target design is deliberately the same. The differences are mostly data, not structure — Fort Collins / Greeley / Loveland communities in place of the Yampa Valley, a 277-broker roster, six offices (including Greeley and, already, Steamboat), and a far deeper Market Data library than the Steamboat production site carries. Where a row diverges from the Steamboat mapping, the note says so.

One scope note: this inventory works at the content-bucket / navigation level (the site's information architecture), not every individual blog post, report, or listing. Listings, issues, and posts flow through the buckets — settle the buckets first.

02The five-disposition rubric

Every piece of Fort Collins content gets exactly one of these. When you find a page this document didn't list, run it through the same five questions and it lands cleanly. Identical rubric to the Steamboat framework, so the two read the same way.

Direct

There is a section on the new homepage that this content is. It moves in with light copy edits. Ask: "Does a homepage section already exist for this?"

Deeper

Belongs on a sub-page reached from the nav; the homepage only teases or links to it. Ask: "Is this a whole page the header should point to, not a homepage block?"

Transform

The information survives but must be rewritten into the new editorial voice before it fits. Ask: "Does this need a new frame, not just a new coat of paint?"

Orphan

No slot in the current design. Requires a decision: retire it, or build a page for it. Ask: "If we shipped the mockup today, where would this literally go? Nowhere?"

Net-new

The reverse case — the new design invents this and Fort Collins has nothing to fill it. It needs original content created. Ask: "Is this block empty until someone writes it?"

05Orphan report — content with no home yet

These are the decisions the mockup forces. Each is real Fort Collins content (or a real function) the new single-page design has no slot for. For each: a recommendation, but the call is yours.

The Market Data library

Market Reports · Quarterly Stats · Annual Report · Concession Reports

A whole top-level section — years of monthly reports, quarterly data, the annual report, and concession data. The mock has only a "Stories" teaser. This is the biggest single gap and the sharpest difference from Steamboat's thinner resources set.

Recommend: build a deeper reports/insights hub behind Stories with a collection + article template. High priority to retain — it's a real SEO and credibility asset.

Communities

14 NoCo neighborhood / town guides

Community pages are major buyer-intent and SEO assets. The thematic Lifestyle section doesn't replace geographic town pages — and the FC set (Ault → Windsor) is entirely different from Steamboat's.

Recommend: build a deeper "Communities" section; link from Lifestyle or Search. Build the template once, populate per market. High priority.

Join Our Team

Recruiting — FC-specific

A full recruiting narrative around the ownership model. No home in the mockup, and no counterpart in the Steamboat framework — this one is unique to the Fort Collins nav.

Recommend: a deeper Careers page (brand story + culture + apply). Agent recruiting is a core driver; worth a real destination, not a footer link.

Commercial real estate

Service line — sale & lease listings

An entire service line with its own listings. The new voice is residential and lifestyle-led; commercial has no tone-appropriate slot.

Recommend: spin off to its own deeper page (or micro-site) linked from footer/Services — don't force it into the editorial narrative.

About The Group

Company story · Leadership · GFP Companies

The brand story survives only as fragments (Hero, GroupGives). There's no place that tells who The Group is, and About also parents Leadership, the 50-year history, and GFP Companies / Additional Services.

Recommend: build a deeper About page gathering the brand statement, ownership model, leadership, offices, and affiliated companies.

Mortgage & property search stack

Group Mortgage · IDX · Log In · Featured listings

Group Mortgage (utility + loan officers) has no editorial home. And the homepage inversion — leading with buyers, never showing a listing — leaves Featured Properties, saved search, and Log In without a slot.

Recommend: fold Mortgage into the buyer guide (Deeper); keep the IDX/account as a deeper "Search / Listings" experience; confirm the buyers-not-listings inversion before building.

06Net-new content the design needs

The reverse gap: sections the mockup invents that Fort Collins has nothing to fill. These aren't migrations — they're content that has to be written or produced before the new site can ship honestly. Same list as Steamboat, retuned for NoCo.

Content that must be created

  • Net-new  Buyers Now — live buyer briefs. The signature idea: real, anonymized buyers with budget, story, and a representing agent. Reverse-prospecting content with no source on the Tribus site. Needs a sourcing + writing + approval workflow (and the "Read the brief" / inquiry-form pipeline behind it).
  • Net-new  NoCo lifestyle taxonomy + photography. The mock's Ski/Ride/Rest/See/Stay and its imagery are Steamboat's. Fort Collins needs its own frame (e.g. Old Town, foothills & trails, CSU, breweries, family neighborhoods) and hero-grade local photography to match the art direction.
  • Net-new  Agent filter facet. The People grid filters agents by season (Deep Winter → Aspen Turn) — a Steamboat concept. FC brokers have no season tags; decide the FC facet (specialty, community, language) and populate a new data field for all 277.
  • Net-new  Editorial voice pass. Hero line, eyebrows, buyer-brief copy, and the migrated market/first-time/why-Steamboat-equivalent content must be rewritten to the new voice — budget a copy/editorial pass, not just a paste.
  • Net-new  Buyer & Seller guides. "Precision" and "Fluency" cards link to guides that don't exist as such yet — Why Buy / Why Sell / How We Market are the raw material but need rewriting into single guides.

08Recommended next steps

  1. Confirm the three big inversions

    Buyers-not-listings on the homepage, flat agent grid (no leadership hierarchy), and editorial-over-utility tone. These drive every orphan decision — lock them once, for both markets, before building deeper pages.

  2. Rule on each orphan in §5

    For the Market Data library, Communities, Join Our Team, Commercial, Mortgage, About, and the IDX/account stack: decide retire vs build a deeper page. That turns this framework into a real sitemap.

  3. Settle the shared-vs-market data model

    Offices (6 incl. Greeley & Steamboat), the 14 NoCo communities, the 277-broker roster with a new filter facet, phone numbers, and affiliation badges are all data that differs from Steamboat. Decide what's shared and what's per-market before templating.

  4. Commission the net-new content (§6)

    Buyer briefs, a NoCo lifestyle frame + photography, the agent filter facet, and the buyer/seller guides gate launch — start them in parallel with the migration.

  5. Migrate bucket by bucket, voice-editing as you go

    Work the matrix top to bottom. Every "Transform" row is a copy task, not a copy-paste — and Fort Collins carries the heaviest Transform load in Market Data. Keep compliance disclaimers (MLS/IRES/REALTOR®/accessibility) non-negotiable in the footer.
