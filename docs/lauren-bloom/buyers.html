For buyers

Buying here, in four steps.

Most buyer guides run to ten steps because they are describing paperwork. This one describes decisions — the four that actually change the outcome.

The process

Four decisions, in order.

The Group's full buyer process sits behind this — escrow, inspection, appraisal, closing. These are the parts where you and Lauren do the thinking.

  1. 01

    Decide your number, not your approval

    A lender will pre-approve you for more than you want to spend. Set the monthly payment you are comfortable with first, then get pre-qualified against it. In the valley, budget the things a Front Range buyer forgets: plowing, propane, a longer drive to everything, and HOA dues that in some subdivisions carry a water tap.

  2. 02

    Pick the market before the house

    Steamboat, Stagecoach, Oak Creek, Phippsburg, Hayden and Clark are six different trade-offs between price, commute, water and covenants. Choosing the wrong one and then hunting for a house inside it is the single most common way buyers waste a season. Lauren will talk you out of an area if it is wrong for you.

  3. 03

    Settle the ground questions before earnest money

    For anything with land attached: recorded year-round access, well permit class, septic feasibility, buildable envelope after setbacks and slope, and whether the county would allow a second dwelling. These are answerable in days and they are the difference between a good parcel and an expensive view.

  4. 04

    Write an offer that reads as serious

    Price is one term of several. Inspection window, financing contingency, closing date, post-closing occupancy, what stays with the house. In a thin market the strongest offer is often not the highest one — it is the one the seller believes will actually close.
