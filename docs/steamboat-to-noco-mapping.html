01How to read this

The other two documents each map one current site onto the new 1000WATT mockup. This one is different: it sets the two current sites against each other. It answers a question that comes before the redesign — how much of what The Group publishes is already shared between markets, and how much is genuinely local? The answer decides how much of the new build can be one template versus two.

Both sites are, underneath, the same kind of thing — a TRIBUS/IDX platform organized around menus of tasks. But they are not the same size. Fort Collins is the larger, older, deeper site: a 277-broker roster, six offices, a full top-level Market Data library, a recruiting track, and 14 Northern Colorado community guides. Steamboat is the smaller resort-market site, with its own things Fort Collins doesn't carry. Notably, Fort Collins already lists a Steamboat office — the two are not strangers.

Direction of the read: the matrix walks the Steamboat buckets first and names the Fort Collins counterpart for each, so Steamboat-only means "no counterpart on the NoCo site." §6 then flips it and lists what lives only on Fort Collins. Scope note: this works at the content-bucket / navigation level, not every listing, report, or post.

02The five-relationship rubric

Every Steamboat content bucket gets exactly one relationship to its Fort Collins counterpart. Same five chip colors as the two 1000WATT mappings, re-cast for a site-to-site read.

Shared

Both sites carry it and mean the same thing. A 1:1 counterpart that could be one page for both markets. Ask: "Is this the same content wearing two addresses?"

Richer on FC

Both have it, but Fort Collins carries a deeper, fuller, or larger version. Steamboat's is the lighter cut. Ask: "Same idea — is NoCo's just bigger?"

Market-specific

Both have the slot, but the content is inherently local and differs — communities, lifestyle, offices, agents, MLS board. Same template, different data. Ask: "Same shelf, different goods?"

Steamboat-only

Present on the Steamboat site, absent on Fort Collins. No counterpart on the NoCo nav. Ask: "Does the other site simply not do this?"

Fort Collins-only

The reverse — present on Fort Collins, absent on Steamboat. Catalogued in §6. Ask: "Is this a NoCo-only capability Steamboat never built?"

05Steamboat-only content — no Fort Collins counterpart

The short list of things the Steamboat site does that the Fort Collins site does not. Small in number, but real — mostly a function of the resort market. If the two ever merge onto one platform, these need a decision: keep as Steamboat-market pages, or drop.

Property Mgmt & Vacation Rentals

Resort service line

A distinct service — managing and renting second homes — that only makes sense in a resort market. Fort Collins has no equivalent offering anywhere in its nav.

Keep as: a Steamboat-market service page. Don't force it into a shared template; it's genuinely local.

Forecast

Market tool

A forward-looking market forecast. Fort Collins has a deeper historical data library but no forecast tool — so this is Steamboat's one data asset NoCo lacks.

Decide: promote it into the shared reports/insights hub (it would strengthen NoCo too), or retire if lightly used.

Second-Home Buyer focus

Buyer audience

Steamboat's buyer education targets second-home buyers; Fort Collins targets first-time buyers. Neither audience page exists on the other site.

Keep both: they're market-appropriate. A shared buyer guide can carry both audiences as branches.

Sold Homes view

Listings utility

Steamboat gives sold listings a named menu item as proof-of-work. Fort Collins leaves solds to search filters only.

Low stakes: both are just IDX filter states. Standardize one approach across markets in the new Search.

06Fort Collins-only content — no Steamboat counterpart

The reverse gap, and the longer list — Fort Collins is the deeper site. These are capabilities and content areas Steamboat's site simply never built. If The Group unifies on one design, most of these are worth extending to Steamboat rather than dropping.

On Fort Collins, absent on Steamboat

  • FC-only  The Market Data library. A whole top-level section — Quarterly Statistics and Seller Concession Reports in particular have no Steamboat equivalent. The single biggest asymmetry between the two sites.
  • FC-only  Join Our Team. A full recruiting track built around the ownership model. Steamboat has no careers/recruiting content at all.
  • FC-only  New Construction. A builder directory (Argenta, Baessler, Sage, Savant, Stonefield, Thrive, TruMark…). No Steamboat counterpart.
  • FC-only  How We Market Your Home. A dedicated seller-marketing page; Steamboat folds this (thinly) into Sell.
  • FC-only  Our Global Reach. The LPI syndication story told as a seller benefit — Steamboat carries LPI only as an affiliation badge.
  • FC-only  Scale. 277 brokers and six offices, with agent filters by Designation & Language. Steamboat's roster and office footprint are a fraction of that.
  • FC-only  GFP Companies / Additional Services. Related-company links off About with no Steamboat parallel.

08Recommended next steps

  1. Treat the platform decision as one decision

    Both sites run on TRIBUS and both are headed for the same 1000WATT design. Whatever is decided about IDX, accounts, and the buyers-not-listings inversion applies to both markets at once — decide it once.

  2. Adopt Fort Collins as the depth baseline

    FC is the richer site — Market Data, recruiting, New Construction, seller-marketing. Build those templates from the FC content and extend them to Steamboat, rather than rebuilding thin Steamboat versions.

  3. Draw the shared-vs-local line (§7)

    Guides, Search, People, reports, Stories, offices → one shared build. Communities, Lifestyle, GroupGives, affiliations, compliance → authored per market. Lock this split before templating.

  4. Reconcile the overlap that already exists

    Fort Collins already lists a Steamboat office. Sort out which site owns Steamboat agents, listings, and offices before migration, so the same broker doesn't live in two rosters.

  5. Keep the genuinely-local content local

    Property Management & Vacation Rentals (Steamboat) and the 14 NoCo community pages (Fort Collins) are market-specific by nature. Don't over-unify — a shared template with local data beats a lowest-common-denominator page.
