Areas of expertise

Routt County is not one market.

Twenty minutes of driving changes the price per acre, the water situation, the covenants and the buyer pool. Here is how Lauren reads each corner of it.

The areas

Six places, six different questions.

Steamboat Springs

Ski-in condominiums, Old Town Victorians and the Yampa Street riverfront. The most liquid market in the county and the most competitive — here the question is usually how fast, not whether.

Stagecoach

Twenty minutes south, big views, and lots you can still buy outright. Read the covenants and confirm the water tap before you fall in love with a parcel.

Oak Creek

A real town with a real main street. Where most of Lauren's land and ranchette work happens, from quarter-acre infill on Seneca Trail to eighty-acre holdings above the valley.

Phippsburg & Yampa

South Routt ranching country, and where Lauren lives. Older houses on generous lots at prices that stopped existing further north around 2019.

Hayden

The valley's growth story — the airport, new subdivisions off Hawthorne, and the best commute-to-price ratio in the county.

Clark

Elk River Valley: Steamboat Lake, Hahn's Peak, and cabins that trade on privacy rather than proximity. Winter access is the first question, not the last.

Ranch & land

The questions nobody asks until closing.

A parcel is a set of permissions, not a picture.

Most land deals in South Routt go sideways for the same handful of reasons, and none of them appear in the listing. Whether there is a legal, recorded, year-round access. Whether the well permit is household-use-only or allows livestock. Whether the buildable envelope survives the setbacks once you account for slope. Whether the county will let you put a second dwelling on it, ever.

Lauren's twenty years in landscape design and horticulture were spent answering the physical half of that list — soil, water, drainage, what will grow and what will slide. The regulatory half came with six years of Routt County closings. Buyers get both before they go under contract, not after.

  • Access and easement review before earnest money
  • Well permit class and augmentation requirements
  • Slope, soils and septic feasibility
  • Zoning, density and second-dwelling potential
  • Covenant enforcement, in practice not on paper
  • Ditch, irrigation and grazing considerations
