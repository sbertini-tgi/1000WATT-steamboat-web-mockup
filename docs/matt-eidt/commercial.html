Commercial & investment

The half of the market nobody markets.

Steamboat's commercial and mixed-use inventory is small, traded quietly, and priced on assumptions that are hard to check from outside the valley.

How it trades

Small inventory, thin comparables.

There are only so many retail bays on Lincoln Avenue, only so many industrial units at Copper Ridge, and in a given year only a handful of either changes hands. That means the comparable set for any commercial property here is small enough that a single unusual sale distorts it — and small enough that the people who closed those sales are the only reliable source on why the numbers looked the way they did.

The same is true of the investment side. A short-term-rental condominium is priced off projected income, and projections are where the optimism lives. Matt underwrites on actual occupancy and actual expenses: management split, HOA reserves, cleaning, the shoulder seasons nobody models, and whether the licence transfers at all.

  • Downtown retail, office and mixed-use
  • Copper Ridge and light industrial
  • Short-term-rental underwriting on real occupancy
  • Licence transferability and HOA rental caps
  • 1031 exchange timing and coordination
  • Owner-user versus investor analysis
